L01 Project details

A launch film that sells the weather, not the floor plan.

Oriel Bay Holdings asked for a film that would open the sales gallery for a forty level tower on the harbour wall. The brief allowed two minutes and no voice over.

We treated the building as a lighthouse that people live in. The film never shows a kitchen or a price. It shows what the tower does to the light around it, and lets a buyer imagine standing inside that light.

L03 Storyline

A tower that keeps the harbour light.

Noa grew up watching the harbour light from her grandmother's balcony. The light is gone now and the tower stands on its footing.

The film follows one evening as she moves into level 31. We see what she sees: the sun leaving the water, the windows coming on below her one by one, the red mast lamp taking over the job the old light used to do.

L04 Directorial intent

Concrete and tide, cut to one rhythm.

The tower turns two degrees at every level. We cut to the same idea: each shot holds a little longer than the one before, and the camera turns a little further, until the last shot pulls back into the fog.

Every plate was logged against the tide table, so the reflections in the edit always agree with the clock.

L05 Creative references

Tide tables, long exposures, a section drawing.

The look came from places a property film rarely looks: harbour tide tables, long exposure night photography of breakwaters, and the elevation drawings of the tower itself.

We kept the drawing on the monitor during the shoot. When a frame stopped reading as the drawing, we moved the camera until it did.
